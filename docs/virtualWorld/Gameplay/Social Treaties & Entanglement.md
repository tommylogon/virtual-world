# Social Treaties and Entanglement

## Beyond Isolated Relationships

In standard game engines, relationships are modeled as isolated pairwise scores (-100 to +100 closeness between Actor A and Actor B). This fails to capture realistic social dynamics, where individuals live inside webs of mutual treaties, domestic leverage, collective loyalties, and shared secrets.

In VirtualWorld, social interaction is governed by **Social Treaties and Relational Entanglement**.

## Core Types of Social Dynamics

### 1. Multi-Party Treaties and Pacts
Explicit or implicit agreements formed by groups of characters to manage mutual risk:
- **The "No Secrets" Treaty**: An agreement between intimate partners or close friends to disclose plans openly, removing the burden of second-guessing and hidden rendezvous.
- **Non-Rival Solidarity**: Agreements between friends (such as Zoe Mercer and Sammy Lopez) to prioritize their foundational friendship over competition for romantic attention. Jealously is acknowledged, talked through on bathroom floors, and managed rather than allowed to fester into sabotage.
- **Mutual Alibis and Cover Stories**: Coordinated deception created to survive external threats (such as Sammy's friends backing her "all-girl hangout" cover story to protect her from parental wrath regarding neck bruises).

### 2. Domestic Leverage and Power Asymmetry
Teenage and young-adult characters do not exist in autonomous vacuums. They operate under the constant weight of domestic authority and economic dependence:
- **Parental Surveillance and Purges**: Zoe facing her mother Helen staging her bedroom for real-estate open houses, threatening her macabre art collection and emotional sanctuary.
- **Disciplinary Stakes**: Sammy navigating the fury of her parents, where failing Chemistry or showing signs of intimacy risks grounding, losing her skateboard, or facing physical punishment.
- **Financial Sovereignty**: Using independent Etsy earnings or odd jobs to buy deadbolts, art supplies, or car parts, systematically eliminating parental leverage.

### 3. Somatic and Relational Tells
Interpersonal tension does not merely produce dialogue; it triggers involuntary bodily and behavioral tells:
- Pulling hoodies up to hide collarbones or bruises.
- Avoiding eye contact in school hallways when bullies whisper.
- Shifting posture, checking door locks, tapping metronomes, fidgeting with necklaces.
- Freezing when physical touch approaches an unresolved emotional or biological boundary.

## Simulation Principles for Social Mechanics

1. **No Artificial Player Deference**:
   NPCs do not abandon their values, domestic fears, or prior treaties to flatter the human player. If the player demands an action that violates an NPC's treaty or endangers their family standing, the NPC resists, negotiates, or shuts down.

2. **Secrets Carry Weight and Propagation Risk**:
   Information known by one character has a propagation radius. Characters weigh the risk of disclosure against loyalty, fear of exposure, and personal gain.

3. **Treaties Form Through Shared Crisis**:
   Treaties are earned through shared vulnerability (sitting on cold bathroom floors, defending someone in a hallway, working side-by-side in a garage) rather than superficial dialogue options.
