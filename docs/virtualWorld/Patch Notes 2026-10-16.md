# The Commons, two bug fixes, and a faster world — 16 October 2026

**Written to be read cold by anyone who has never seen this project.** No code, no
file names, no jargon.

---

## The short version

The Commons — the shared place where characters talk — now has two ways of being a
community. Two bugs you could actually hit are fixed: starting a scenario used to wipe
out a character's personality and appearance, and the Commit button could rename your
scenario by accident. And the world now loads in pieces around the character you're
playing, so turns feel snappier.

---

## The Commons: Live social, or Town board & letters

The Commons is the shared messaging space where the scenario's characters talk. It
already saves its conversations with the world and starts paused, so opening it does
not begin anyone's turns.

Now it offers two ways of being a community:

- **Live social** — the full Commons: a public conversation, private group chats, and
  one-to-one direct messages, each with its own read state.
- **Town board & letters** — public notices and private letters only. Group chats are
  disabled, and the tone is that of a town that passes notes rather than one that
  holds court.

Pick one when you set up the scenario. Both are immediate Commons communication — the
world does not simulate physical delivery of a letter.

---

## Starting a scenario no longer erases who a character is

This one was quiet and easy to miss, which is why it sat unfixed. When you started a
scenario, every character came onto the screen with their personality and appearance
blank — no description, no character voice — even though the world had all of it
written down somewhere else. The auto-dress system, which picks outfits, was reading
that blank space too.

It is fixed. A character now loads with their real personality and appearance, the
outfit picker sees it, and the prompt a character speaks from carries their actual
voice. A scenario written the old way still loads correctly.

---

## The Commit button no longer renames your scenario

The top bar shows your scenario name, and next to it a Commit button when you have
uncommitted changes. The name is also clickable — tap it to rename the scenario.

Those two sat in the same box, and clicking Commit used to bubble into the rename
control. So if you clicked Commit and then typed without looking, you could rename
the scenario instead of committing it. The click now stops where it means to. The
rename control still works exactly as before.

---

## The world loads in pieces, so turns feel snappier

Nothing about the look of the game changed. What changed is what happens behind the
scenes while a character is taking their turn: instead of loading the whole world at
once, the engine now asks for just the slice around the character who is acting.

The upshot is that the turn queue does not stall and the graph does not redraw on
every tick. If you have been in a scenario with a lot of characters, that is the thing
you would notice. There is no new panel, no new button, no new setting — this one is
entirely underneath the surface.
