# Long-Horizon Pursuits

## Tangible Projects Over Abstract Tasks

In simple game loops, character activity is episodic: a character performs a quick one-turn action ("craft bandage", "talk to guard") and resets to an idle state.

In VirtualWorld, human lives are anchored by **Long-Horizon Pursuits**: multi-day or multi-week material projects that demand physical tools, persistent labor, scheduled time blocks, and emotional stamina.

```
Long-Horizon Pursuit (e.g. Rebuild 1998 Dodge Ram)
  ├── Calendar Window: After school (16:00 - 19:00), Weekends
  ├── Physical Tools: Socket wrench, PB Blaster, Jack stands, Chilton manual
  ├── Spatial Anchor: Jake's Garage / Driveway
  ├── Social Entanglement: Sammy & Jake working together; grease on knuckles
  └── Incremental Milestones:
        [1] Free seized manifold bolts (done)
        [2] Flush dry transmission fluid (in progress)
        [3] Replace cracked serpentine belt (pending)
```

## Anatomy of a Long-Horizon Pursuit

Every major pursuit consists of five concrete pillars:

### 1. Material and Tool Requirements
Pursuits require real physical artifacts present in the world. Progress cannot be faked without them:
- **Silicone and Latex SFX Portfolio**: Requires sculpting clay, armature wire, liquid latex, pigment powders, heat curing lamps, and a clean workspace (Zoe's bedroom workshop).
- **Truck Restoration**: Requires a socket wrench set, penetrating oil (PB Blaster), torque wrench, replacement belts, and shop rags (Jake's driveway).
- **DIY Skate Mini-Ramp**: Requires 3/4-inch plywood sheets, 2x4 framing lumber, galvanized screws, Henrik's carpentry tools, and backyard clearance.

### 2. Temporal Grounding and Calendar Milestones
Pursuits exist inside the simulation's calendar (starting Friday, October 14, 2016). They must fit around daily life:
- High school class hours (08:00 - 15:30).
- Evening homework and family dinners (17:30 - 19:00).
- Dedicated weekend labor sessions (Saturday afternoons).
- Explicit deadlines: December 1 for college portfolio early submissions, regional skate jam competitions, or end-of-semester GPA cutoffs.

### 3. Incremental Physical Progress
Progress is recorded as verifiable physical state changes, not abstract percentages:
- Sculpting 10 distinct prosthetics: 5 fantasy monster sculpts completed, 5 anatomical trauma appliances remaining.
- Academic recovery: completing stoichiometry problem sets on Tuesdays and quadratic equations on Thursdays.
- Heavy-metal guitar demo: tracking metronome speed increases and recording clean takes of lead solos onto a digital recorder.

### 4. Interpersonal Entanglement
Pursuits serve as social catalysts:
- Working side-by-side on a truck provides an unforced context for vulnerable romantic confessions without awkward staring.
- Tutoring sessions create physical proximity and intellectual respect between mismatched peers.
- Helping a younger sibling (Toby) build a science fair diorama creates a protective buffer against domestic neglect.

### 5. Interruptions and Friction
Real life constantly threatens to derail pursuits:
- Parents demanding household chores or staging rooms for sale.
- Running out of PETG filament or stripping a crucial bolt.
- Severe physical exhaustion, skate slams, or emotional burnout.
A stalled pursuit does not disappear; it remains an active cognitive pressure in GIMP and MOSIAC until resumed, replanned, or surrendered.
